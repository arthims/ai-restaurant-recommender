# Swiggy Instamart User Research Validation

This document compiles the qualitative user research findings conducted to validate the feedback insights identified in Phase 1. It details the cohort definition, interview guide, and simulated interview transcripts of six quick-commerce users in India.

---

## 1. Cohort Definition: "The Routine Replenisher"
To ground our research, we targeted users belonging to the **"Routine Replenisher"** segment.
*   **Definition**: Users who order from Swiggy Instamart at least 3 times a week, but purchase from 2 or fewer distinct product categories (typically "Dairy & Bread" and "Munchies & Beverages").
*   **Behavioral Anchor**: High checkout speed, utility focus, and heavy usage of "Buy Again" widgets.

---

## 2. Interview Guide
The qualitative interviews were guided by the following core questions:
1.  *How do you typically build your cart on Swiggy Instamart? Walk me through your steps.*
2.  *When was the last time you bought something from a category you don't usually visit (e.g., Pet Care, Stationery, Gourmet, Personal Care)? What triggered that purchase?*
3.  *What prevents you from buying fresh produce (fruits, vegetables, meat) on the app?*
4.  *How do you feel about the homepage layout? Do you ever browse the categories listed?*
5.  *If you want to try a new item or premium brand, what details do you look for, and what makes you hesitate?*

---

## 3. Simulated User Interview Transcripts

### Interview 1: Habitual Lock-in & Checkout Speed
> **Respondent**: Aarav M., 27, Software Engineer, Bangalore  
> **Order Profile**: 4 times/week (Dairy & Bread, Munchies)  
>
> *"I open Swiggy, click Instamart, and immediately tap the 'Buy Again' horizontal scroll bar. I add milk, eggs, bread, and maybe a bottle of Diet Coke. Then I swipe to pay. The whole thing takes under 45 seconds. I don't think I've ever scrolled down to see the other sections. Why would I? I'm trying to get checkout done as fast as possible. If I need something else, I search for it directly. I don't browse."*

### Interview 2: Quality Trust Deficit
> **Respondent**: Priya K., 31, Marketing Manager, Mumbai  
> **Order Profile**: 3 times/week (Packaged Goods, Dairy)  
>
> *"I order chips, cheese, biscuits, and chocolates from Instamart. But I will never buy fresh chicken, fish, or green leafy vegetables here. Once I ordered spinach, and it arrived completely yellow and wilted. For fresh vegetables and meat, I prefer my local Safal vendor or local butcher where I can touch the food and verify it's fresh. I'm not risking my family's health on 10-minute delivery convenience."*

### Interview 3: UI Clutter & Navigation Friction
> **Respondent**: Dev N., 23, College Student, Delhi  
> **Order Profile**: 5 times/week (Late-night Snacks, Beverages)  
>
> *"The Instamart homepage is a chaotic mess. There are flash sale counters, spin-and-win wheel banners, card-specific cashback banners, and sponsored brands. It's so visually noisy that it actually hurts my eyes. I never browse categories because they are buried below all this promotional garbage. I just type whatever snack I want in the search bar and check out. If search doesn't show it immediately, I close the app and open Blinkit."*

### Interview 4: Trial Risk & Package Sizes
> **Respondent**: Sneha R., 29, HR Consultant, Hyderabad  
> **Order Profile**: 3 times/week (Dairy, Pantry Staples)  
>
> *"I saw this new organic cold-brewed lavender tea category on Instamart last week. I was curious to try it, but it only came in a pack of 6 bottles for 450 rupees. What if I don't like the taste? That's a waste of money. I searched for a single-bottle trial option, but it wasn't available. They need to have trial sizes. I'm not spending hundreds of rupees to test a brand I've never heard of."*

### Interview 5: Information Deficit on New Categories
> **Respondent**: Vikram S., 34, Product Analyst (Father of an Infant), Bangalore  
> **Order Profile**: 4 times/week (Groceries, Household Cleaners)  
>
> *"I wanted to buy baby massage oil from the baby care section. The app showed a new organic Ayurvedic oil brand. But the product page had only one image of the front box, and the description just said 'Natural baby oil'. There was no ingredient list, no safety warnings, and no details on whether it's suitable for newborns. I didn't want to risk skin rashes on my baby, so I canceled the order and bought it from a physical pharmacy."*

### Interview 6: Category Visibility & Search Reliance
> **Respondent**: Rohit J., 26, Graphic Designer (Dog Owner), Pune  
> **Order Profile**: 3 times/week (Snacks, Dairy)  
>
> *"I didn't even know Swiggy Instamart sold pet supplies! I've been using the app for a year. I always thought it was just for groceries, sodas, and ice cream. Yesterday, my dog ran out of pedigree treats, and on a whim, I typed 'dog treats' in the search bar, and it popped up. If I hadn't searched, I would have assumed they didn't have it. The app category menu is totally invisible."*

## 4. Key Findings Summary Matrix

| Friction Theme | Primary Barrier | Impact on Category Exploration | MVP Solution Hook |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Habitual Lock-in** | Search-and-go speed | User never visits category menus. | Conversational AI adds items contextually based on cart. |
| **Quality Trust Deficit** | Fear of bad fresh produce | Restricts buying to packaged categories. | Quality assurance highlights and local sourcing badges. |
| **UI Navigation Bloat** | Homepage ads and visual noise | Hides minor categories from view. | Clean, direct context cards on checkout page. |
| **Trial Risk** | Large minimum packet sizes | Users avoid experimenting due to cost. | Gamified "Insta-Discovery Wheel" offering trial samples/coupons. |
| **Information Deficit** | Missing specifications/ingredients | Stops purchases of baby, skin, and pet care. | Detailed product sheets and AI summaries of reviews. |
